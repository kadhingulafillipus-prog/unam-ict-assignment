## 1. Introduction and Learning Objectives
Welcome to this educational learning website designed to support Grade 10 English as a Second Language (ESL) learners in developing narrative writing skills. This resource guides learners through understanding how stories are planned, structured, and executed.

### Measurable Learning Objectives
By the end of this web unit, learners will be able to:
1. Identify at least five important narrative elements (Setting, Characters, Plot, Conflict, Climax, Resolution).
2. Organise story ideas into a clear chronological beginning, middle, and ending structure.
3. Develop clear plot conflicts, situational climaxes, and satisfying resolutions.
4. Apply suitable linking words, varied sentence structures, and sensory descriptive language to write a coherent narrative essay of 200–250 words.

## 2. Learning Content & Core Structure
A narrative is a story that presents events in a logical sequence. It may be based on real or imagined events.

### The 5 Connected Plot Stages
1. **Exposition:** Introduces characters, physical setting, and initial situation.
2. **Rising Action:** Builds the central problem or internal/external friction.
3. **Climax:** The biggest moment, highest tension, and central turning point.
4. **Falling Action:** Shows what happens next as a result of the climax.
5. **Resolution:** Ends the story where the problem is solved or a lesson is learned.

### Language Tools
* **Linking Words:** First, Then, Next, Afterwards, Meanwhile, Later, Suddenly, Eventually, Finally.
* **Descriptive Language:** Focus on the five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch) to make settings vivid.
* **Sentence Variety:** Mix simple, compound, and complex sentence formations to enrich flow.
