## 1. Introduction and Learning Objectives
Welcome to this educational learning website designed to support Grade 10 English as a Second Language (ESL) learners in developing narrative writing skills. This resource guides learners through understanding how stories are planned, structured, and executed.

### Measurable Learning Objectives
By the end of this digital unit, learners will be able to:
1. Identify and define at least five important narrative elements (Setting, Characters, Plot, Conflict, Climax, and Resolution).
2. Organise story ideas into a clear chronological beginning, middle, and ending framework.
3. Develop clear plot conflicts, situational climaxes, and satisfying structural resolutions.
4. Apply suitable linking words, descriptive sensory language, and varied sentence structures to write a coherent narrative essay of 200–250 words.

## 2. Learning Content & Core Structure
A narrative is a story that presents events in a logical sequence. It may be based on real or imagined events.

### The 5 Connected Plot Stages
1. **Exposition:** Introduces the setting and characters.
2. **Rising Action:** Builds the central problem or conflict.
3. **Climax:** The biggest moment and most important turning point.
4. **Falling Action:** Shows what happens next as a result of the climax.
5. **Resolution:** Ends the story where the problem is solved or a lesson is learned.

### Language & Paragraphing Tools
* **Paragraphing:** Divide your text into a **Beginning** (introduce context), **Middle** (develop action and climax), and **Ending** (show the final result).
* **Linking Words:** Chronological items like *First, Then, Next, Afterwards, Meanwhile, Later, Suddenly, Eventually, Finally*.
* **Descriptive Language:** Use the five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch) to make your setting vivid.

### Situation Prompt
*You are walking home from school when you notice a small bag lying beside the road. You pick it up. Suddenly, you hear someone calling your name...*

### Planning Task
Learners must complete the planning fields below on the live web framework before beginning their writing task to structure their ideas:
* **Setting:** Where and when?
* **Characters:** Who is involved?
* **Conflict:** What is the main problem?
* **Rising Action:** What happens before the turning point?
* **Climax:** What is the most important moment?
* **Resolution:** How is the problem solved?
